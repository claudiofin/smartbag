# SmartBag — what has to be certified, and what nobody has asked yet

⚠️ **Generated by `tools/compliance.py` from `hardware/bom.py` and
`thermal/budget.py`.** The equipment table is derived, so it cannot drift from
the design. The rest is a list of what to go and find out.

⛔ **Nothing here is legal advice and no clause number is quoted unless it is
certain.** A wrong standard reference in a compliance plan is worse than a blank
one: it gets believed, budgeted and scheduled, and the mistake surfaces at the
lab. Where the applicable standard depends on a judgement a test house makes,
this says so.

## 2 · Europe

The product is radio equipment, so it falls under the **Radio Equipment
Directive (2014/53/EU)**. Under RED a manufacturer self-declares against
harmonised standards, and where no harmonised standard covers a case, a
**notified body** has to be involved.

| what | status |
|---|---|
| **2.4 GHz BLE** | ✅ well-trodden. A wideband data transmission module in this band is the most tested thing in consumer electronics, and the SoC vendor supplies test reports for its own radio. The *product* still has to be measured — the antenna is on this board, next to this ground plane, inside leather. |
| **60 GHz radar ×2** | ⛔ **the open question.** 57–64 GHz is licence-exempt in the EU for short-range devices, but which harmonised standard applies to a pulsed coherent radar in a consumer bag — and whether one applies at all — is a judgement for a test house. **Ask Acconeer first**: they sell the A121 into consumer products and will know what route their customers take, and whether any of it transfers. |
| **EMC** | applies. Immunity and emissions for a radio product. |
| **RF exposure** | applies. Two radars and a BLE radio against a body, in a bag carried against a hip. The powers are tiny; the assessment still has to exist. |
| **Electrical safety** | applies to the product as a whole, including the charging path. |
| **Battery** | the cell is already **UN 38.3** and **IEC 62133-2** certified by Jauch — that is on its datasheet. ⚠️ The finished product is a separate matter. |
| **RoHS / REACH** | applies. Every part in `hardware/bom.py` is a current catalogue part from a major distributor, so compliance data exists for all of them — but it has to be collected. |
| **WEEE** | applies. It is an electronic product with a built-in battery. |

## 4 · Wireless power

The receiver is a **BQ51013BRHLR** — a standard WPC 5 W receiver —
so any Qi pad speaks its language.

- ⚠️ **Using the Qi logo requires WPC membership and certification of the
  receiver.** Without it the product still works on Qi pads; it just cannot be
  called Qi in marketing or on the box.
- ⛔ **Foreign object detection is not certified, it is calibrated.** The
  transmitter compares the receiver's quality factor against a number the
  receiver declares, and that number has to be measured on the assembled bag.
  See `tools/calibrate.py`. A bag that declares a Q it does not have is a bag
  that charges with a coin on it.

## 5 · The order this actually happens in

⭐ **The 60 GHz question comes first and it is the only one that can kill the
design.** Everything else is cost and schedule; that one is a yes or a no, and
it wants answering before five prototypes are ordered rather than after.

1. **Ask Acconeer.** What route do their consumer customers take in the EU and
   the US, what test reports exist for the A121, and what transfers to a product
   that embeds it. One email, and it may answer most of section 2 and 3.
2. **Ask one test house for a scoping call.** They will want the table in
   section 1. That call is usually free and it produces the real standards list
   and a quote.
3. **Then order the prototypes** — the radiated measurements need a built board
   in a built bag, so the sample is the input to everything above anyway.
4. Battery paperwork, RoHS/REACH declarations, WEEE registration: these are
   administration and they follow the technical work.

## 6 · What this means for the schedule

⚠️ **Certification is measured in months and thousands, not days.** A full RED
route for a product with an unusual radiator is the longest single item between
here and a shipped box — longer than tooling the bag, longer than the PCB lead
times, longer than the firmware.

⛔ **And it is not in any plan in this repository.** That is the finding, and it
is why this file exists.
